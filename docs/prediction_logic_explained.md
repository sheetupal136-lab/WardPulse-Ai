## 2. Answers to the 12 Specific Audit Questions

### 1. What exact column is the training target?
The exact training target column is **`monthly_complaint_demand`** in `data/municipal_ward_complaints_training.csv`.

### 2. How is the 30-day forecast target constructed?
It was constructed by aggregating all historical complaints in `hack-day-lucknow-datasets/p3-ward-civic-demand/grievances_public_wards.csv` (which contains 556,561 records from February 2020 to June 2025 across 65 calendar months) and dividing each ward's total complaints by 65:
$$\text{monthly\_complaint\_demand} = \frac{\text{total\_complaints}}{65}$$
For Ward 4 (Yelahanka Satellite Town), there were $4,426$ total complaints logged over 65 months:
$$\frac{4426}{65} = 68.0923 \approx 68.1 \text{ complaints/month}$$
The "30-day forecast" is therefore **a prediction of the long-term monthly average complaint demand** for the ward, not an actual time-series forecast for the next 30 calendar days.

### 3. Is the model trained on historical complaint counts or static ward features?
The model is trained **entirely on static ward demographic and infrastructure features** ($X$) to predict the historical average complaint rate ($y$). 
- The features ($X$) consist of 25 static indicators: Census 2011 demographics (population, households, density, decadal growth rates) and BBMP infrastructure counts (road length, streetlights, bus stops, parks, lakes, police stations, schools).
- **No historical complaint counts, no lagged complaint features, and no rolling averages are included in $X$**.

### 4. Does the dataset contain dated complaint records?
- In `data/municipal_ward_complaints_training.csv`: **No**. It is a cross-sectional table with exactly 1 row per ward ($156$ rows total).
- In the raw source dataset (`hack-day-lucknow-datasets/p3-ward-civic-demand/grievances_public_wards.csv`): **Yes**. It contains $556,561$ dated grievance records with timestamps (`grievance_time`) ranging from `2020-02-08 01:01:00` to `2025-06-30 23:59:00`. The current pipeline collapsed all 65 months into a single static average.

### 5. Is 79.3 generated by actual `model.predict()`?
**Yes.** `79.3` (exact float: $79.3402$) is the direct output of `pipeline.predict(X_w4)` executed by the trained `scikit-learn` Ridge pipeline on the 25 static feature attributes of Ward 4.

### 6. How is the baseline 68.1 calculated?
The baseline $68.1$ is the actual historical average monthly complaint volume for Ward 4 recorded in the training data ($4,426 / 65 = 68.09$). In `backend/app/services/ml_service.py` line 398, when a prediction is requested for an existing ward with historical records, the backend extracts:
$$\text{baseline\_demand} = \text{round}(\text{ward\_row}[\text{"monthly\_complaint\_demand"}], 1) = 68.1$$
The model prediction of $79.3$ is then compared against this historical average ($79.3 - 68.1 = +11.2$, or $+16.5\%$).

### 7. How are model evaluation metrics calculated?
In `backend/app/services/ml_service.py` lines 246–277:
1. The 156 wards are split randomly into a **training set** (124 wards, 80%) and a **held-out test set** (32 wards, 20%) using `train_test_split(..., test_size=0.2, random_state=42)`.
2. The pipeline is fitted exclusively on the 124 training wards.
3. Predictions $\hat{y}_{\text{test}}$ are generated on the 32 unseen test wards:
   - $\text{MAE} = \frac{1}{32} \sum_{i=1}^{32} |y_i - \hat{y}_i| = \mathbf{16.84}$
   - $\text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{32} \sum_{i=1}^{32} (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2} = \mathbf{23.66}$
   - $R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2} = \mathbf{0.767}$
4. For baseline comparison, a naive dummy mean predictor ($\bar{y}_{\text{train}} = 56.03$) is evaluated on the same 32 test wards:
   - $\text{Baseline MAE} = \mathbf{33.33}$
   - $\text{Baseline RMSE} = \mathbf{49.28}$
   - The Ridge model achieves a **49.5% error reduction** over the baseline.

### 8. Is validation performed on unseen wards or unseen future dates?
Validation is performed on **unseen wards** (spatial holdout), **NOT on unseen future dates**.
- The 32 test wards were held out from training, confirming that the model generalizes to new geographical areas.
- However, because the target was averaged over the entire 2020–2025 window, there is **no temporal out-of-time validation** (e.g., training on 2020–2023 and testing on 2024–2025).

### 9. How is Critical Priority determined?
In `backend/app/services/ml_service.py` lines 463–495:
The backend computes predictions $\hat{y}$ across all 156 wards and computes the empirical quartiles:
- **75th Percentile ($q_{75}$):** $63.9$ complaints/month
- **50th Percentile ($q_{50}$):** $42.2$ complaints/month
- **25th Percentile ($q_{25}$):** $30.7$ complaints/month

A ward is classified as:
- **Critical Priority:** $\hat{y} \ge 63.9$ (Top 25% highest complaint demand)
- **Elevated Priority:** $42.2 \le \hat{y} < 63.9$
- **Moderate Priority:** $30.7 \le \hat{y} < 42.2$
- **Low Priority:** $\hat{y} < 30.7$

Because Ward 4 has $\hat{y} = 79.3 \ge 63.9$, it falls in the top quartile of predicted workload and is assigned **Critical Priority**.

### 10. How are feature importance percentages generated?
In `backend/app/services/ml_service.py` lines 286–295:
For Ridge regression, the pipeline extracts the standardized linear model coefficients $\beta_i$:
$$\text{Importance}_i = \frac{|\beta_i|}{\sum_{j=1}^{25} |\beta_j|}$$
They reflect the relative magnitude of the standardized linear weights in the regression equation and sum to $100\%$.

### 11. Why is the confidence interval N/A?
In `frontend/src/context/WardPulseContext.tsx` line 443, before a user explicitly triggers the prediction inference by clicking the **"Generate Prediction"** button, the initial ward object had `uncertaintyRange: null`. When rendered on the card before generation, the template rendered:
`{uncertainty ? ... : 'N/A'}`
Once the user clicks **"Generate Prediction"**, the backend `/api/ml/predict` endpoint dynamically computes the genuine 95% confidence interval using the validation residual standard deviation ($\sigma_{\text{res}} = 23.51$):
$$\text{Margin} = 1.96 \times 23.51 = 46.08$$
$$\text{Lower Bound} = \max(0, 79.3 - 46.1) = \mathbf{33.2}$$
$$\text{Upper Bound} = 79.3 + 46.1 = \mathbf{125.4}$$
The interval $[33.2, 125.4]$ is returned by the backend; it only appeared as "N/A" on un-executed UI states.

### 12. Is 77% reliability simply rounded $R^2$?
**Yes.**
- The model test $R^2 = 0.7671$.
- In `WardPulseContext.tsx`, `accuracy = Math.round(res.metrics.r2 * 100) = 77`.
- Calling $R^2 \times 100$ "Reliability" is a UI presentation label. In statistical machine learning, $R^2$ is the coefficient of determination (the fraction of target variance explained by the model), **not** an accuracy or calibration percentage.

## 4. Model Limitations & Critical Issues for Hackathon Presentation

When presenting this project to technical hackathon judges, several key structural discrepancies between this current cross-sectional model and the official competition specification must be acknowledged and addressed.

### Discrepancy 1: Static Aggregation vs. 18-Month Spatio-Temporal Forecasting
- **Current Model:** Predicts a single timeless scalar: the 5-year average monthly complaints.
- **Hackathon Requirement (Problem 03: Ward Pulse):** The competition requires forecasting monthly complaint counts for **40 specific cold-start wards** across **18 distinct calendar months** (January 2024 to June 2025) and across **6 category groups** (`electrical`, `solid_waste`, `roads`, `trees`, `animals_health`, `other`), generating a total of:
  $$40 \text{ wards} \times 18 \text{ months} \times 6 \text{ categories} = 4,320 \text{ prediction cells}$$
- **Limitation:** The current model cannot predict seasonal surges (e.g., monsoon road damage in July vs. dry winter months).

### Discrepancy 2: Evaluation Metric (WAPE vs. $R^2$)
- **Current Model:** Evaluates on $R^2 = 0.767$, $\text{MAE} = 16.84$, $\text{RMSE} = 23.66$.
- **Hackathon Requirement:** The official evaluation metric is **WAPE** (Weighted Absolute Percentage Error):
  $$\text{WAPE} = \frac{\sum_{w, m, c} |y_{w,m,c} - \hat{y}_{w,m,c}|}{\sum_{w, m, c} y_{w,m,c}}$$
  - **Lazy Baseline:** $\text{WAPE} \le 0.58$
  - **Good Baseline:** $\text{WAPE} \le 0.45$
  - **Excellent Baseline:** $\text{WAPE} \le 0.38$
- In addition, the competition evaluates **Mean Spearman Rank Correlation** ($\ge 0.85$) and **Top-25% Recall** ($\ge 0.85$).

### Discrepancy 3: Category Disaggregation
- **Current Model:** Predicts total aggregate complaints across all categories combined.
- **Hackathon Requirement:** Municipal crews are specialized (electrical linemen vs. road resurfacing contractors vs. garbage sanitation trucks). The competition requires separate predictions for each of the 6 category groups.

### Discrepancy 4: Spatial Lag & Neighbor Attributes Ignored
- **Current Model:** Uses only the ward's own internal Census and infrastructure features.
- **Hackathon Requirement:** Wards do not exist in isolation. Civic complaints spill over borders (e.g., garbage dumping on ward boundaries, arterial roads traversing multiple wards). Incorporating spatial lag features (complaints in the 5 nearest neighboring wards) is specifically highlighted in the competition guide.
